Buying criteria

  • Choose layout before brand: full-size, TKL, 75 percent, or 60 percent.
  • Linear switches are usually easier for gaming; tactile switches are a good general-purpose middle ground.
  • Hot-swap is useful if you want a repairable or moddable board.
  • Avoid software-dependent features unless the software has a good reputation.

Common mistakes

  • Buying blue/clicky switches for a shared room.
  • Choosing a 60 percent board without realizing it hides arrow and function keys.
  • Assuming RGB means better build quality.
  • Ignoring keycap size and replacement compatibility.

FAQ

Are blue switches good for gaming?

They work, but they are loud. Linear or tactile switches are usually easier to live with.

Is hot-swap required?

No. It is nice for repairs and switch experiments, but a solid non-hot-swap board can still be the better budget buy.
