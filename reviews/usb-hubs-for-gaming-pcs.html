Best USB hubs for gaming PCs

A USB hub for a gaming PC is not glamorous, but it can stop your desk from becoming a cable cave. The right hub depends on what you plug in: controllers and dongles are easy, external drives need speed, and power-hungry devices may need a powered hub instead of a tiny passive splitter.

Quick verdict

For most gaming desks, use a simple hub for low-power peripherals and a powered hub for many devices, charging ports, or anything that disconnects under load. Do not expect a cheap hub to behave like Thunderbolt or a full dock.

Buying criteria

  • Match port speed to device: storage needs faster ports than controllers.
  • Choose powered hubs for many devices or anything that draws meaningful power.
  • Cable length and mounting matter on a gaming desk.
  • Keep mouse and keyboard direct to the PC if you are sensitive to competitive input reliability.

Common mistakes

  • Buying a tiny passive hub for too many devices.
  • Ignoring whether ports are USB 2.0, 3.x, or USB-C.
  • Expecting display output from a basic hub.
  • Mounting the hub where cables yank on the port.

Final take

For gaming desks, prioritize stable ports and sensible cable placement. Choose powered hubs for many devices or anything power-hungry.

FAQ

Do USB hubs add input lag?

A decent hub should be fine for normal peripherals, but competitive players may prefer direct motherboard ports for keyboard and mouse.

Should external drives use a hub?

They can, but storage is where port speed and hub quality matter most. Use a quality powered hub or a direct motherboard port for important drives.
